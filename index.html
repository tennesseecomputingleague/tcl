(05) Curriculum

What students will learn.

Programming follows a step-by-step path from first line of code to finished project. Theory follows the official ACSL topics, so students are ready to compete.

Programming track Python · Google Colab
  1. 01 Intro to computer science
    What CS is and where it shows up in everyday life, plus the basic vocabulary.
  2. 02 Algorithms & sequencing
    Algorithms as step-by-step instructions. Students write their own for everyday tasks.
  3. 03 Intro to programming
    Variables, data types and operators, written as real Python in Google Colab.
  4. 04 Conditionals & loops
    How programs make decisions with if/else, and repeat work with loops.
  5. 05 Functions & abstraction
    Organizing code into reusable functions so programs stay clean and short.
  6. 06 Build a game or quiz
    Students make an interactive game or quiz using everything so far.
  7. 07 Testing & debugging
    Common errors, how to find them, and how to make code better.
  8. 08 Intro to AI models
    How AI models learn from data, what they can and can’t do, and how to use them responsibly.
  9. 09 Build your own AI model
    Students collect data, train a simple model of their own, and test how well it makes predictions.
  10. 10 Show & tell
    Review the big ideas. Students present their projects and explain their code.
Theory track Follows the ACSL categories
  • Number systems 1011₂ · 7B₁₆
  • Boolean algebra ¬(A+B)
  • Digital electronics AND · NAND · XOR
  • Bit-string flicking RSHIFT-1 10101
  • Recursive functions f(n−1) + f(n−2)
  • Prefix / infix / postfix 5 8 3 1 − / +
  • What does this program do? trace the code
  • Data structures stacks · queues · trees
  • Graph theory paths · cycles

(06) FAQ

Questions from parents.

01 Who can join?

Any Tennessee student in grades 5 through 8. Total beginners are welcome, and experienced coders get harder challenges so they keep growing.

02 How much does it cost?

Learning with TCL is free: weekly lessons, monthly workshops and all materials. Students who want to compete pay just $20 for the whole year. That one fee covers competition costs, and competing is optional.

03 What does my student need?

Their own laptop and a Google account. A parent’s Google account is fine. They will also need internet access for the Sunday Zoom sessions.

04 What programming language will they learn?

Python. We code in Google Colab, which runs in the web browser, so there is nothing to install.

05 When does it start?

The first class is November 28. Registration closes November 21.

06 When and where are sessions?

Online sessions are on Zoom every Sunday around 11 AM and last 90 minutes. Once a month there is a 60-minute in-person workshop at the John P. Holt Brentwood Library (8109 Concord Road).

07 What happens in a session?

Three parts: hands-on Python programming, a short lesson on how computers work (like hardware vs. software), and a theory segment (like binary numbers or Big-O).

08 How does competing work?

Competing is optional. Students who want to compete pay $20 once for the entire year. They prepare for the American Computer Science League (ACSL) and take part in two TCL competitions a year.

09 Who runs TCL?

TCL is an independent, student-led initiative. Students build and teach the curriculum, and parents and adult advisors provide oversight.

10 How will we get updates?

Everything official goes in Google Classroom. We also post on Instagram at @tennessee_computing_league. Questions? Email tennesseecomputingleague@gmail.com.
